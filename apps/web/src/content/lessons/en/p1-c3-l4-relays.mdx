# Relays – Electrically Controlled Switches

In this lesson, you'll learn how relays work as electrically controlled switches, allowing low-voltage circuits to control high-voltage loads safely.

<LessonChapter title="What You'll Learn" id="what-youll-learn">

- Relay coil and contact structure
- NO (Normally Open) and NC (Normally Closed) contacts
- Electrical isolation between control and load circuits
- Safe control of high-voltage loads from Arduino

<InfoBox type="info">
  A relay is an electromagnetic switch. A small control current energizes a coil, which mechanically flips a switch to control a separate circuit.
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="Relay Structure" id="relay-structure">

### Main Components

1. **Coil**: Electromagnet that creates the switching action
2. **Armature**: Moving metal piece attached to the switch
3. **Contacts**: The actual switch connections
4. **Spring**: Returns contacts when coil is de-energized

### How It Works

1. Apply voltage to the coil terminals
2. Coil creates a magnetic field
3. Armature is pulled, moving the contacts
4. Load circuit is connected/disconnected

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="Contact Types" id="contact-types">

### Common Terminal

The **COM** (Common) terminal is the switching point.

### Normally Open (NO)

- **NO** contact is disconnected when relay is off
- Connects to COM when relay is energized
- Use for: Turning things ON with Arduino

### Normally Closed (NC)

- **NC** contact is connected when relay is off
- Disconnects from COM when relay is energized
- Use for: Fail-safe applications, turning things OFF

### SPDT (Single Pole Double Throw)

Most relays have all three terminals:
- 1 COM, 1 NO, 1 NC
- The switch toggles between NO and NC

<InfoBox type="tip">
  NC contacts are useful for safety systems - if power fails, the circuit remains closed (e.g., emergency stop circuits).
</InfoBox>

<LessonChapter title="Electrical Isolation" id="electrical-isolation">

### Why Isolation Matters

The coil circuit and contact circuit are **completely separate**:
- Arduino can safely control 120V/240V AC appliances
- No electrical connection between control and load
- Protects your microcontroller from high voltages

### Safety Considerations

- Use appropriate relay ratings (voltage and current)
- Keep high-voltage wiring separate from low-voltage
- Use relay modules with optocouplers for extra isolation

<InfoBox type="warning">
  Working with mains voltage (120V/240V) is dangerous! Always follow proper safety procedures and local electrical codes.
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>
