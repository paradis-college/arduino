# Inductors – Coils That Resist Change

In this lesson, you'll learn how inductors work - coils of wire that oppose changes in current and store energy in magnetic fields.

<LessonChapter title="What You'll Learn" id="what-youll-learn">

- Inductor symbol and how they work
- How inductors oppose current changes
- Energy storage in magnetic fields
- Why switch-off voltage spikes occur

<InfoBox type="info">
  An inductor is a coil of wire that creates a magnetic field when current flows through it. This field resists any change in current.
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>

### Symbol and Units

Inductors are measured in **Henrys (H)**:
- **mH** (millihenrys) = 10⁻³ H
- **µH** (microhenrys) = 10⁻⁶ H

### How Inductors Work

1. When current starts flowing, the inductor opposes the increase
2. When current is established, the inductor acts like a wire
3. When current tries to decrease, the inductor tries to maintain it

<InfoBox type="tip">
  Think of an inductor like a heavy flywheel - it takes effort to get it spinning, but once spinning, it wants to keep going.
</InfoBox>

### Back-EMF

When you try to change the current through an inductor, it creates a voltage (back-EMF) that opposes the change:

**V = L × (di/dt)**

Where:
- V = induced voltage
- L = inductance in Henrys
- di/dt = rate of current change

### Rising Current

When current increases:
- Magnetic field builds up
- Energy is stored in the field
- Inductor opposes the increase

### Falling Current

When current decreases:
- Magnetic field collapses
- Stored energy is released
- Inductor tries to maintain current flow

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="Switch-Off Voltage Spikes" id="voltage-spikes">

<GifStep
  stepNumber={3}
  title="Add a Flyback Diode"
  gifUrl="https://placehold.co/600x400/2563eb/white?text=Flyback+Diode+GIF"
  alt="Adding flyback diode for protection"
>
  Connect a diode in reverse across the inductor (cathode to positive). When current is cut, the diode provides a safe path for the collapsing energy.
</GifStep>

### The Danger

When you suddenly cut current to an inductor:
1. Current can't stop instantly (inductors resist change)
2. The collapsing magnetic field induces a high voltage
3. This **voltage spike** can damage transistors and ICs!

### Protection

Use a **flyback diode** in parallel with the inductor:
- Provides a path for current when switched off
- Safely dissipates the stored energy
- Essential for motors, relays, and solenoids

<InfoBox type="warning">
  Always use flyback diodes when switching inductive loads! The voltage spike can be many times higher than the supply voltage.
</InfoBox>
