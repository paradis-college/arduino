# Motors – Turning Electricity into Motion

In this lesson, you'll learn how motors convert electrical energy into mechanical motion, and how to control them with Arduino.

<LessonChapter title="What You'll Learn" id="what-youll-learn">

<LessonChapter title="DC Motors" id="dc-motors">

### How They Work

DC motors spin continuously when voltage is applied:
- **Forward rotation**: Apply positive voltage
- **Reverse rotation**: Reverse the voltage polarity
- **Speed**: Proportional to voltage/PWM duty cycle

### Speed Control with PWM

PWM varies the average voltage by rapidly switching on and off:
- 0% duty cycle = Motor stopped
- 50% duty cycle = Half speed
- 100% duty cycle = Full speed

### Current Requirements

Most DC motors need more current than Arduino can provide directly. Use a transistor or motor driver!

<LessonChapter title="Servo Motors" id="servo-motors">

### What Makes Servos Different

Servo motors have built-in:
- **Gearbox**: Increases torque
- **Position sensor**: Knows current angle
- **Control circuit**: Maintains desired position

### Position Control

Servos respond to PWM signals differently:
- **1ms pulse**: 0° position
- **1.5ms pulse**: 90° position (center)
- **2ms pulse**: 180° position

Pulses repeat every 20ms (50Hz).

### Standard vs Continuous Rotation

- **Standard servo**: Moves to specific angle (0-180°)
- **Continuous rotation servo**: Spins like a DC motor

<InfoBox type="tip">
  Servos are perfect for projects needing precise positioning, like robotic arms, pan-tilt cameras, or door locks.
</InfoBox>

<LessonChapter title="Direction Control" id="direction-control">

### H-Bridge Concept

To reverse a DC motor's direction, you need an H-Bridge circuit:
- Four switches arranged in an "H" pattern
- Allows current to flow in either direction through the motor
- Motor driver ICs (L298N, L293D) contain H-bridges

### Arduino Motor Shield

For easier control, use a motor shield that includes:
- H-bridge drivers
- PWM speed control
- Current sensing
