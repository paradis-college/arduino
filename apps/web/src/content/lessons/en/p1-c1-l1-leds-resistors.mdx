# How LEDs and Resistors Work

In this lesson, you'll learn the fundamentals of LEDs and resistors - two of the most essential components in electronics.

<LessonChapter title="What You'll Learn" id="what-youll-learn">

- Basic voltage, current, and resistance (V/I/R) relationships
- LED polarity (anode and cathode)
- Why LEDs need series resistors
- How to calculate resistor values for 5V and 3.3V systems (~220Ω)

<InfoBox type="info">
  An LED (Light Emitting Diode) is a semiconductor device that emits light when current flows through it in the forward direction.
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="Understanding Voltage, Current, and Resistance" id="understanding-vir">

### Ohm's Law

The relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) is defined by Ohm's Law:

**V = I × R**

- **Voltage (V)** - Electrical pressure, measured in Volts
- **Current (I)** - Flow of electrons, measured in Amperes (A)
- **Resistance (R)** - Opposition to current flow, measured in Ohms (Ω)

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="LED Polarity" id="led-polarity">

LEDs have polarity - they only work when connected in the correct direction:

- **Anode (+)** - The longer leg, connects to positive voltage
- **Cathode (-)** - The shorter leg, connects to ground

<InfoBox type="warning">
  Connecting an LED backwards won't damage it, but it won't light up either. Always check the polarity!
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>

<LessonChapter title="Why LEDs Need Resistors" id="why-resistors">

LEDs have very low internal resistance. Without a current-limiting resistor, too much current will flow and destroy the LED.

### Calculating Resistor Value

For a typical LED:
- Forward voltage drop: ~2V
- Safe operating current: ~20mA (0.02A)

For a **5V system**:
```
R = (5V - 2V) / 0.02A = 150Ω (use 220Ω for safety)
```

For a **3.3V system**:
```
R = (3.3V - 2V) / 0.02A = 65Ω (use 100Ω or 220Ω for safety)
```
