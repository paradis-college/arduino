### Symbol and Units

Capacitors are measured in **Farads (F)**, but most practical capacitors are:
- **µF** (microfarads) = 10⁻⁶ F
- **nF** (nanofarads) = 10⁻⁹ F
- **pF** (picofarads) = 10⁻¹² F

### Types

- **Ceramic capacitors** - Small values, non-polarized
- **Electrolytic capacitors** - Large values, polarized (+ and - terminals)

<InfoBox type="warning">
  Electrolytic capacitors have polarity! Connecting them backwards can cause damage or even explosion.
</InfoBox>

### Charging Behavior

When connected to a voltage source through a resistor:
1. Initially, current flows quickly (capacitor acts like a short circuit)
2. As the capacitor charges, current decreases
3. Eventually, current stops (capacitor is fully charged)

### Discharging Behavior

When the voltage source is removed:
1. The capacitor releases its stored energy
2. Current flows in the opposite direction
3. Voltage decreases exponentially to zero

### The Formula

The **time constant** (τ) determines how fast a capacitor charges or discharges:

**τ = R × C**

- τ (tau) is in seconds
- R is resistance in ohms
- C is capacitance in farads

### Timing Reference

- After **1τ**: Capacitor is ~63% charged
- After **5τ**: Capacitor is ~99% charged (considered fully charged)

### Example

With R = 10kΩ and C = 100µF:
```
τ = 10,000Ω × 0.0001F = 1 second
Full charge time ≈ 5 seconds
```
