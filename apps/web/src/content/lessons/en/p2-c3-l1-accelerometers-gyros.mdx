<LessonChapter title="Tilt Switch Introduction" id="tilt-switch">

### Simple Tilt Detection

Before complex IMUs, simple tilt switches detected orientation:
- Contains a metal ball inside a tube
- Ball rolls to make/break contact
- Digital output: tilted or not

### Limitations

- Only detects ON/OFF states
- Can't measure angle or acceleration
- Bouncy and imprecise

This is why we use accelerometers for better tilt sensing!

<LessonChapter title="Accelerometer Basics" id="accelerometer-basics">

### What It Measures

Accelerometers measure acceleration in 3 axes:
- **X-axis**: Left/Right
- **Y-axis**: Forward/Backward
- **Z-axis**: Up/Down

At rest, an accelerometer measures gravity (1g ≈ 9.8 m/s²).

### Tilt from Gravity

When tilted, gravity distributes across axes:
- **Flat**: X≈0, Y≈0, Z≈1g
- **Tilted left**: X positive, Z less than 1g
- **Tilted forward**: Y positive, Z less than 1g

### Angle Calculation

```cpp
float roll = atan2(accelY, accelZ) * 180 / PI;
float pitch = atan2(-accelX, sqrt(accelY*accelY + accelZ*accelZ)) * 180 / PI;
```

<LessonChapter title="Gyroscope Basics" id="gyroscope-basics">

### What It Measures

Gyroscopes measure rotational velocity (degrees per second):
- **X-axis rotation**: Roll rate
- **Y-axis rotation**: Pitch rate
- **Z-axis rotation**: Yaw rate

### Combining with Accelerometer

Accelerometers are accurate long-term but noisy during motion.
Gyroscopes are smooth but drift over time.

**Sensor fusion** combines both for best results!

<InfoBox type="tip">
  The MPU6050 is a popular, affordable 6-axis IMU with both accelerometer and gyroscope. It connects via I2C.
</InfoBox>

</LessonChapter>
